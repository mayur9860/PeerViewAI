# The Art of Academic Hedging

Hedging is not weakness. It is precision. When you write "our results suggest" instead of "our results prove," you are accurately representing the epistemological status of your findings.

There are three levels of hedging. Modal verbs ("may," "could," "might") are the lightest. Evidential phrases ("the data suggest," "our findings are consistent with") are moderate. Full disclaimers ("within the limitations of our sample size") are the strongest.

Match your hedging level to your evidence strength. Avoid double-hedging: "It is possible that the data might suggest" weakens your point to meaninglessness. One hedge per claim is usually sufficient.
