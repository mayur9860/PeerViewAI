# Writing Clear Methods Sections

A methods section should be reproducible. Another researcher with access to the same materials should be able to replicate your experiment by following your methods section alone.

Common clarity failures include: omitting software versions, failing to specify statistical thresholds, not stating sample sizes, and using ambiguous time references like "samples were incubated briefly."

Structure your methods chronologically: sample preparation, experimental procedure, data collection, analysis pipeline. Use subheadings liberally. A methods section with five clear subsections is easier to follow than one unbroken block of text.

When describing statistical analyses, state the test, the software, the significance threshold, and any corrections for multiple comparisons.
