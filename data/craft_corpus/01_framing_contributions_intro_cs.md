# Framing Your Contributions Clearly

The introduction of a computer science paper must answer one question above all others: what is new here? Reviewers will scan your intro looking for an explicit contributions statement, typically signaled by phrases like "our contributions are" or "we make the following contributions."

A common mistake is burying the contribution deep in the introduction after two pages of background. Instead, state the problem in the first paragraph, hint at your approach in the second, and list contributions by the third or fourth paragraph.

Each contribution should be a concrete, falsifiable claim. Instead of "we study X," write "we show that X achieves Y improvement on benchmark Z." Concrete claims give reviewers something to verify in the results section.

If your paper has multiple contributions, number them. Numbered lists are easier to scan and make it harder for reviewers to miss one of your points.
