# Synthesis vs. Listing in Related Work

The most common weakness in related work sections is the "wall of citations" pattern: "Smith (2020) did X. Jones (2021) did Y. Lee (2022) did Z." This tells the reader nothing about how these works relate to each other or to your contribution.

Instead, organize related work thematically. Group papers by approach, by problem variant, or by the limitation your work addresses. For each group, explain the shared methodology, the common limitation, and how your work differs.

A strong related work section tells a story: here is what the field has tried, here is what works, here is what does not, and here is the gap that your paper fills. The final paragraph should explicitly connect back to your contributions.

Use comparison tables when appropriate. A table with columns for method, dataset, key result, and limitation can replace several paragraphs of prose and is far easier for reviewers to scan.
