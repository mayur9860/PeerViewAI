# Separating Results from Discussion

A common structural error is mixing interpretation with observation. The results section should state what you found; the discussion section should explain what it means.

In results, report data objectively. Save the interpretation for the discussion. Figures and tables belong in results. Each figure should have a descriptive caption that lets it stand alone.

If you find yourself writing "this suggests" or "this is likely because" in your results section, that sentence probably belongs in the discussion. Keep results factual and let discussion be analytical.
